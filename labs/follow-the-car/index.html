The network saw a pattern.

A date, time, plate, vehicle image, and camera location can be ordinary records. Repeated observations can also reveal where a vehicle appeared and when. The privacy question is not only what one camera records—it is what connected records can reveal together.

What Flock says

Flock's current LPR policy says LPR data can include plate and vehicle images, plate number and state, vehicle characteristics, date, time, and camera location. It says customer queries are audit-logged and that LPR data is hard-deleted on a rolling 7-day basis by default, subject to customer law or policy. Read the policy ↗

What critics worry about

The ACLU argues that networked ALPR systems can create broad location-surveillance risks because repeated observations and data sharing can expose patterns of movement at scale. Read the ACLU analysis ↗

Questions worth asking locally

  • How long is data retained?
  • Who can search it, and are searches audited?
  • Which agencies or networks can receive shared data?
  • What happens after a low-confidence or incorrect read?
  • What public policy governs use, access, and oversight?
