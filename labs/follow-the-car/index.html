What happens when the data is wrong?

In Plymouth, Minnesota, in June 2026, Flock cameras twice flagged a vehicle after a stolen-plate record had been entered incorrectly into a national database. The driver was confronted by multiple police vehicles and patted down before officers determined the underlying database entry was wrong.

Safeguards Flock says it uses

Flock's current LPR policy says LPR data can include plate and vehicle images, plate number and state, vehicle characteristics such as color and make, date, time, and camera location. Flock also markets vehicle-description search using attributes including make, model, color, body type, and other visible characteristics. It says customer queries are audit-logged and that LPR data is hard-deleted on a rolling 7-day basis by default, subject to customer law or policy. Read the policy ↗

Why civil-liberties critics remain concerned

The ACLU argues that networked ALPR systems can create broad location-surveillance risks because repeated observations and data sharing can expose patterns of movement at scale. Read the ACLU analysis ↗

Questions worth asking locally

  • How long is data retained?
  • Who can search it, and are searches audited?
  • Which agencies or networks can receive shared data?
  • What happens after an incorrect plate read, vehicle match, or database entry?
  • What public policy governs use, access, and oversight?
