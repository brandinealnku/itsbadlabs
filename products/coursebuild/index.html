The problem

Designing a course and building it are not the same job.

Instructors can know exactly what they want students to learn and still lose hours turning that design into pages, assignments, discussions, quizzes, modules, rubrics, outcomes, dates, points, and repeated LMS updates.

01 · REPETITION

Too much work is mechanical.

Good course design gets buried under copying, configuring, sequencing, and updating the same kinds of objects over and over.

02 · FRAGMENTATION

The course exists in too many places.

Syllabi, spreadsheets, source files, assignment plans, Canvas objects, rubrics, and due dates all have to stay aligned.

03 · RISK

Speed without control is not useful.

AI can generate quickly, but course changes need reviewability, boundaries, duplicate prevention, and a way to recover when something is wrong.

How CourseBuild works

From course intent to Canvas structure.

CourseBuild treats course production as a workflow, not a chat box. The goal is to help instructors move from a plan to an operational course without surrendering control of the academic decisions.

STEP 01Connect the course

Start from the course environment and the materials that define what should be built.

STEP 02Select the operation

Create, update, inventory, audit, or adjust the course instead of rebuilding everything manually.

STEP 03Review the proposed work

Use controlled workflows and dry-run patterns before high-impact changes.

STEP 04Push the course forward

Apply the approved structure, then inspect and refine what changed.

Why it is different

The product is not “AI writes your course.”

The stronger idea is operational: use AI and automation to reduce the parts of course production that do not require an instructor’s expertise, while making the consequential actions visible and reviewable.

Instructor control

The system supports the person responsible for the course rather than pretending to replace their academic judgment.

Course-aware workflows

Operations are built around real LMS objects, dependencies, updates, and readiness—not generic generated text.

Safety by workflow

Review, dry runs, duplicate prevention, and recovery-oriented patterns matter because a fast wrong change is still a wrong change.
